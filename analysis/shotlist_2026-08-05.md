# Field shot list — 128 images, 2026-08-05 (summer, one day)

Covers what is capturable today: sun/shade, altitude, occlusion, surface,
buggy perspective, negatives, and a night LED block after dark. Rain, snow,
winter and fog are not available today — see issues #7–#11.

## Before anything: downwash check (not counted)

Hover the Neo 2 at 2 m then 1 m for 30 s over a plot seeded with 10 cigarette
butts and 2 wrappers. Photograph the layout before and after.

**If the prop wash moves the objects, every low drone shot below is compromised**
— switch those blocks to handheld-at-height and note it. Do this first.

## Kit

- Neo 2, batteries charged, 4K
- ~60 cigarette butts (mixed: fresh, weathered, crushed, burnt, filter-up/down)
- 3 bottles, 3 cans, 5 wrappers, 2 plastic bags, 2 paper items
- A **scale reference** — a coloured card of known size, in frame
- **Centre markers** — bright tape crosses or coloured pegs
- Confusers: pine cones, stones, twigs, bark, dead leaves
- Notebook or voice memo for altitude / angle / surface / lighting per shot

## Block A — Altitude ladders · 30 images

An altitude ladder is one physical scene shot from three heights with
**nothing moved between shots**. Same butts, same light, same background, three
known scales. It is the direct experimental test of the optics table.

### The three rungs

| Rung | Height | Frame width | Butt size | Represents |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Close** | 0.2 m | 0.44 m | 88 px | Buggy grasp view |
| **Mid** | 1 m | 2.2 m | 44 px | Buggy search / drone low |
| **Far** | 2 m | 4.4 m | **22 px** | Drone detection altitude |

**Why "far" is 2 m and not 10 m.** Above ~2.2 m a cigarette butt drops under
20 px and is gone. A 10 m rung would tell you nothing new about the primary
class — it would just be an empty field. 2 m *is* the far end for butts, and it
is exactly where the pipeline's detection pass operates, so it is the most
important frame of the three.

*Optional extra (uncounted):* on ladders A1 and A5 only, add one frame at 10 m.
The butts will be invisible; the can or bottle will not. That contrast is the
whole argument for a two-pass mission, and two frames is enough to document it.

### Scene layout — the "target patch"

The 0.2 m rung is the binding constraint: the frame there is only **0.44 m
wide**, so anything larger is cropped and the ladder breaks. Build each scene
inside a **30 × 20 cm patch**:

- **5 cigarette butts**, 5–8 cm apart, varied orientation (filter-up,
  filter-down, on edge, one partly buried)
- **1 larger item** (can / bottle / wrapper) at the patch edge — it stays
  detectable when the butts do not
- **Scale reference** card, flat, in the patch
- **Centre marker** just outside the patch, so you can frame it from 2 m

### Procedure per ladder

1. Lay out the patch. Photograph it once from ~1 m as a ground-truth layout
   record (not counted).
2. **Close and mid rungs: hold the drone in your hands**, props off, camera
   straight down. Do **not** substitute a phone — the ladder is only valid if
   all three rungs come from the same lens and sensor.
3. **Far rung: fly it.** Hover over the centre marker, camera at nadir (−90°),
   let the aircraft settle before shooting.
4. Keep the patch **centred**. Off-centre objects land in the wide lens's
   distorted periphery, which is a different optical problem.
5. **Log the altitude the aircraft reports**, not the one you intended.
6. Shoot 2–3 frames per rung, keep the sharpest — count one per rung.
7. Do not touch the patch between rungs. If wind or downwash moves anything,
   note it and restart that ladder.

### The ten ladders

Three rungs each. Because they are cheap, the ladders also carry this day's
surface and lighting variation.

| Ladder | Images | Surface | Light | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | 1–3 | Gravel | Full sun | Worst case: butt colour ≈ gravel |
| A2 | 4–6 | Gravel | Shade | Same surface, opposite light |
| A3 | 7–9 | Tall grass | Full sun | Occlusion × scale together |
| A4 | 10–12 | Mown lawn | Full sun | **The control — the easy case** |
| A5 | 13–15 | Asphalt | Full sun | Dark uniform background |
| A6 | 16–18 | Asphalt | Shade | Wet a patch if you can |
| A7 | 19–21 | Bare soil | Full sun | Mid-tone natural clutter |
| A8 | 22–24 | Leaf litter | Dappled | Hardest confuser background |
| A9 | 25–27 | Sand or concrete | Full sun | Bright background |
| A10 | 28–30 | Grass | Golden hour | Long shadows, ~20:00 |

A4 is deliberately the easy case. If butts disappear at 2 m even on mown lawn,
the problem is the optics rather than the background — a result worth having.

### Check the same evening

For each ladder, note whether you can still identify each butt at the 2 m rung.
Predicted: readable but marginal at 2 m, and the larger item obvious. **If butts
are already unreadable at 2 m, every drone altitude in the plan needs lowering
before bulk capture.** That is why this block goes first.

## Block D — Occlusion ladder · 16 images

Same object, progressively more hidden. 4 occluders × 4 levels.

| # | Occluder | Levels |
|---|---|---|
| 65–68 | Tall grass | none / light / partial / heavy |
| 69–72 | Dead leaves | none / light / partial / heavy |
| 73–76 | Loose soil / gravel | none / light / partial / heavy |
| 77–80 | Other litter overlapping | none / light / partial / heavy |

Levels: none = fully visible, light <25 %, partial 25–60 %, heavy >60 % hidden.
**Record the level per shot.**

## Record for every image

Altitude · camera angle · surface · lighting · occlusion level · artificial
light yes/no. Without this the images cannot be split by condition later, and
the point of a structured capture is lost.
