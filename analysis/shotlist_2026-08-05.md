# Field shot list — 128 images, 2026-08-05 (summer, one day)

Covers what is capturable today: sun/shade, altitude, occlusion, surface,
buggy perspective, negatives, and a night LED block after dark. Rain, snow,
winter and fog are not available today — see issues #7–#11.

## Before anything: downwash check (not counted)

Hover the Neo 2 at 2 m then 1 m for 30 s over a plot seeded with 10 cigarette
butts and 2 wrappers. Photograph the layout before and after.

**If the prop wash moves the objects, every low drone shot below is compromised**
— switch those blocks to handheld-at-height and note it. Do this first.

## Kit

- Neo 2, batteries charged, 4K / 60 fps stills-from-video or photo mode
- ~40 cigarette butts (mixed: fresh, weathered, crushed, burnt, filter-up/down)
- 3 bottles, 3 cans, 5 wrappers, 2 plastic bags, 2 paper items
- A **scale reference** — a coloured card or ruler of known size, in frame
- Confusers: pine cones, stones, twigs, bark, dead leaves
- Notebook or voice memo for altitude / angle / surface / lighting per shot

## Block A — Altitude ladders · 35 images

**The highest-value block on the list.** An altitude ladder is one physical
scene, photographed from seven heights, with **nothing moved between shots**.
That gives exact cross-scale correspondence: the same butt, same lighting, same
background, at seven known scales. It is the only way to measure the altitude at
which each class stops being detectable, and it is the direct experimental test
of the optics table in the README.

5 ladders × 7 rungs = 35 images.

### The frame grows 60× from bottom rung to top

This is the constraint that dictates the layout:

| Rung | Height | Frame width on ground | A 30 cm patch is… |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.2 m | **0.44 m** | most of the frame |
| 2 | 1 m | 2.2 m | 14 % of frame width |
| 3 | 2 m | 4.4 m | 7 % |
| 4 | 3 m | 6.6 m | 4.5 % |
| 5 | 5 m | 10.9 m | 2.7 % |
| 6 | 10 m | 21.8 m | 1.4 % |
| 7 | 12 m | 26.2 m | **1.1 %** (≈44 px wide) |

So the scene must be **compact enough to fit inside the 0.2 m frame** — the
bottom rung is the binding constraint, not the top. Anything wider than about
40 × 22 cm is cropped at rung 1 and the ladder breaks.

### Scene layout — the "target patch"

Build each scene inside a **30 × 20 cm patch**:

- **5 cigarette butts**, spread across the patch, 5–8 cm apart, in different
  orientations (filter-up, filter-down, on edge, one partly buried)
- **1 larger item** (can / bottle / wrapper) at the patch edge — this stays
  detectable at the top rungs after the butts have vanished, which is the
  contrast the ladder is designed to show
- **Scale reference** — a coloured card of known size, flat, in the patch
- A **centre marker**: bright tape cross or a coloured peg just outside the
  patch. At 12 m you cannot see the butts on the drone's live view; you need the
  marker to centre the shot.

### Procedure per ladder

1. Lay out the patch. Photograph it once from directly above at ~1 m as a
   **ground-truth layout record** (not counted in the 128).
2. **Rungs 1–2 (0.2 m, 1 m): hold the drone in your hands**, props off, camera
   pointed straight down. Do not substitute a phone — the ladder is only valid
   if every rung comes from the same lens and sensor.
3. **Rungs 3–7 (2, 3, 5, 10, 12 m): fly it.** Hover over the centre marker,
   camera at nadir (−90°), and let the aircraft settle before each frame.
4. Keep the patch **centred** in every frame. Off-centre objects land in the
   lens's distorted periphery, which is a different optical problem.
5. **Log the altitude the aircraft reports**, not the one you intended.
   Barometric and IR altitude drift; a rung logged as "5 m" that was really
   6.2 m corrupts the measurement this block exists to make.
6. Shoot 2–3 frames per rung and keep the sharpest — count one per rung.
7. **Do not touch the patch between rungs.** If wind or downwash moves anything,
   note it and restart that ladder.

### The five ladders

| Ladder | Images | Surface | Patch contents | Why this surface |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | 1–7 | Gravel | 5 butts + 1 can | Worst-case: butt colour ≈ gravel colour |
| A2 | 8–14 | Tall grass | 5 butts, 2 partly occluded, + 1 bottle | Occlusion and scale interacting |
| A3 | 15–21 | Mown lawn | 5 butts + 1 wrapper | High contrast, the easy case — the control |
| A4 | 22–28 | Asphalt / paving | 5 butts + 1 bag | Dark uniform background |
| A5 | 29–35 | Bare soil | 5 butts + wrapper/paper | Mid-tone, natural clutter |

A3 is deliberately the easy case. If butts disappear early even on mown lawn,
the problem is the optics, not the background — and that is a result worth
having.

### What to check the same evening

For each ladder, find the highest rung at which you can still identify each
butt by eye. Predicted: butts readable to ~2 m and gone by 3 m; the can or
bottle still visible at 12 m. **If the real numbers differ, the drone altitudes
in every other block need revising before bulk capture.** That is the entire
purpose of shooting this block first.

## Block D — Occlusion ladder · 16 images

Same object, progressively more hidden. 4 occluders × 4 levels.

| # | Occluder | Levels |
|---|---|---|
| 68–71 | Tall grass | none / light / partial / heavy |
| 72–75 | Dead leaves | none / light / partial / heavy |
| 76–79 | Loose soil / gravel | none / light / partial / heavy |
| 80–83 | Other litter overlapping | none / light / partial / heavy |

Levels: none = fully visible, light <25 %, partial 25–60 %, heavy >60 % hidden.
**Record the level per shot.**

## Record for every image

Altitude · camera angle · surface · lighting · occlusion level · artificial
light yes/no. Without this the images cannot be split by condition later, and
the whole point of a structured capture is lost.
